Flagship 1 – Astrotourism

Dark skies as a resource: astronomy-based tourism that benefits rural communities.

The OAD’s Astrotourism flagship helps rural communities turn their dark night skies into sustainable livelihoods. It supports local guides, small businesses and communities around observatories with free training, practical guides and a community of practice, so that more people share in the benefits of astronomy-based tourism.

  • About 8 in 10 people live under light-polluted skies and cannot see the Milky Way, which makes dark skies rare and valuable
  • A free online course for guides, businesses and anyone curious about the night sky
  • Free guides and workbooks for rural communities, businesses and communities around observatories
  • OAD-funded astrotourism projects from Colombia and Guatemala to Mongolia, Malaysia and the Caribbean; in 2026 a project in Tanzania launched astrotourism tours

Flagship 2 – Astronomy for Mental Health

Looking up together: the night sky for calm, perspective and connection.

Since 2020, the OAD’s Astronomy for Mental Health flagship has used simple, low-cost stargazing activities to support wellbeing in communities. Guided experiences under the night sky help people find calm, a new sense of perspective and connection with others. It supports wellbeing and is not therapy.

  • Open guidelines, free for anyone to use (v1 2023, v2 2025)
  • Facilitator training, including a free online course, and a global community of practice
  • Peer-reviewed findings with Stellenbosch University (IJERPH, 2026), among 9 publications so far
  • Dark-sky retreats, including in Sutherland
  • Wellbeing, not therapy: this is not counselling, assessment, diagnosis, treatment or crisis support, and it does not replace them.

Flagship 3 – Astro 4 Skills: Hack4Dev

Turning young talent into data-science opportunity.

Hack4Dev runs hands-on data science hackathons. Teams of students and young professionals use astronomy-grade data skills to tackle real development challenges. Trained local organisers run 4-day hackathons at their own institutions, so each cycle trains the people who run the next.

  • Launched in 2022 by the OAD with IDIA, DARA, AfAS and BRICS partners
  • 350+ participants selected and trained from about 2,000 applications
  • Women’s participation up from under 20% to 41%
  • 2026: 22 hackathons in 13 countries, with open challenges on GitHub

OAD Innovation Hub

Turning astronomy know-how into businesses and ventures.

The OAD Innovation Hub, now in development, will bridge astronomy, technology and entrepreneurship. It will help ideas and skills from astronomy grow into businesses and ventures, and close the gap between academia and industry.

  • Two tracks: an SME Hub (6 months to 1 year) and a Venture Hub (4 to 6 years)
  • First phase: a call for pitches and the launch of programmes; then working groups and training from 2027
  • Builds on 15 years of OAD funding for astronomy-for-development projects around the world
  • Formal launch at OAD@15, 15–17 February 2027

OAD Research Institute

Building the evidence for how astronomy contributes to development. Pre-launching at this Science Forum.

The OAD Research Institute is envisioned as a distributed global network dedicated to understanding how astronomy contributes to sustainable development. It will generate evidence, share knowledge and build research capacity, drawing on the hundreds of projects the OAD has supported since 2011.

  • Four focus areas: research and evidence, knowledge infrastructure, research capacity building, and global partnerships
  • Pre-launch at Science Forum South Africa on 2 December 2026; formal launch in Cape Town, 15–17 February 2027
  • A Knowledge Portal of projects, data, reports, publications and people
  • A growing network of research affiliates

OAD Funded Projects

Seed funding for projects that use astronomy for a better world.

Through its annual Call for Proposals, the OAD gives seed funding to projects with high potential for positive societal impact using astronomy. The call is open to anyone, anywhere in the world (except where international sanctions prevent payments).

  • 236 projects in 112 countries, 2 million people and €1.4 million, across 13 Sustainable Development Goals (OAD figures)
  • Grants have ranged from €1,000 to €15,000, with an average of about €5,000
  • The call funds projects that use astronomy for societal impact, not astronomy research or activities mainly aimed at promoting astronomy
  • Example: Limpopo Astronomy Public Outreach in South Africa

OAD Regional Offices

Astronomy for development, with a regional focus around the world.

The OAD works through 11 regional offices and language expertise centres around the world. They share the OAD’s objectives with a regional focus, and each one helps select proposals from its region for the OAD Call for Proposals.

  • In Africa: Southern Africa (Copperbelt University, Zambia), East Africa (Ethiopian Space Science and Technology Institute) and West Africa (Centre for Basic Space Sciences, Nigeria)
  • Also: the Andean region, the Arab world, East Asia, Europe, North America, Portuguese-speaking countries, South East Asia, and South West and Central Asia
  • South Africa is served by the Southern Africa office
